01

About my approach

I start with the outcome and the constraint: what has to change, for whom, and what would make the change fail? Then I trace the work backwards from there.

In practice that means customer conversations, messy data, process maps and a bias toward the smallest intervention that can prove something. I'd rather run a narrow test that produces evidence than defend a large plan that produces slides.

I work best with people who care about the problem more than the org chart. I communicate plainly, take responsibility for the outcome, and adapt when the situation calls for it.

I want to work on problems where commercial judgment, business strategy and applied AI are all required — not as separate specialties, but as one way of thinking.

The near-term direction is consulting or an operating role: understanding a business, finding where value is getting lost, and staying close enough to implementation to make the change real.

Longer term, I want to lead — not for the title, but because the decisions I find most interesting are the ones that set direction for everyone else.

  • Fragmented customer information reduces pipeline visibility.

Business value

  • Reduced repetitive prospecting work.

Scope

02 / 05 · Business & Operations

Service Dispatch & Contractor Operations

A service operations platform connecting customer demand with contractor supply through coordinated matching, communication and booking management.

What it is

A service operations platform designed for businesses that connect customers with independent service providers.

The system combines customer intake, contractor acquisition and onboarding, service coverage, job matching, communication and booking confirmation into a coordinated operational process.

Its objective is to help businesses manage both sides of a service marketplace while reducing manual coordination and improving operational visibility.

Business challenges

  • Time-consuming manual coordination between customers and contractors.
  • Difficulty identifying suitable and available service providers.
  • Fragmented communication and inconsistent follow-ups.
  • Limited visibility into contractor readiness and service coverage.
  • Customer requests becoming delayed or unresolved.
  • Difficulty scaling operations without increasing administrative workload.

Business value

  • More efficient coordination of supply and demand.
  • Reduced administrative and operational workload.
  • Better utilization of contractor networks.
  • Greater visibility into service requests and their progression.
  • More consistent customer and contractor experiences.
  • A more scalable service delivery model.

My Contribution

I worked on the front door of the dispatch process: how a service request enters the system and how the customer receives a reference.

Problem

A request creates an expectation, but dispatch can only fulfil it when coverage, contractor readiness and availability line up.

What I did

Built and refined the public intake flow: which request details dispatch receives, how the request is validated, and how the customer receives a confirmation reference.

Scope

I worked on the intake-to-queue handoff; coverage, contractor activation and offer/confirmation sat within the wider Dispatch system.

How the work flows
  1. 01
    Customer request

    Understand what the customer needs.

    Customers submit a service request describing the work they need, their location and relevant preferences. This creates a structured starting point for the business instead of relying on scattered messages or phone conversations.

  2. 02
    Assess requirements

    Understand the job requirements.

    Organize the customer’s request into actionable information, including the type of service, job scope, location and scheduling requirements. This helps the business understand what kind of contractor is needed.

  3. 03
    Check service coverage

    Determine whether demand can be served.

    Evaluate whether the business has suitable service coverage for the customer’s location and requirements. Requests outside current coverage can help identify opportunities for contractor recruitment and future market expansion.

  4. 04
    Contractor acquisition

    Expand the service provider network.

    Identify and recruit contractors based on the services and geographic markets the business wants to support. This helps align contractor acquisition with actual or anticipated customer demand.

  5. 05
    Contractor onboarding

    Understand each contractor’s capabilities.

    Collect relevant contractor information, including offered services, geographic coverage, availability and operating preferences. Structured contractor profiles provide the information needed to identify suitable service providers.

  6. 06
    Contractor activation

    Establish contractor readiness.

    Distinguish contractors who are potential partners from those who are ready to receive service opportunities. This helps the business understand its usable service capacity rather than relying solely on the size of its contractor database.

  7. 07
    Match supply & demand

    Connect the request with suitable contractors.

    Identify eligible contractors based on the customer’s service requirements, location and contractor readiness. This reduces manual searching and helps allocate available service capacity more effectively.

  8. 08
    Coordinate communication

    Manage offers and responses.

    Coordinate communication between customers, contractors and the dispatch team. Contractors receive relevant opportunities and can respond, while the business tracks the progression of each request.

  9. 09
    Confirm the booking

    Establish a clear service commitment.

    Track contractor acceptance and customer confirmation as distinct steps. This gives the business greater visibility into whether a service opportunity has progressed into a confirmed arrangement.

  10. 10
    Exception management

    Resolve requests that need attention.

    Identify requests that cannot progress because of unavailable contractors, expired offers, unanswered communications or coverage limitations. These requests can be reviewed, reassigned where appropriate or handled by the operations team.

  11. 11
    Operations management

    Maintain visibility across the business.

    Centralize customer requests, contractor profiles, communication and booking progression. Give the operations team a clearer understanding of current demand, available supply and outstanding operational decisions.

Connections
  • Customer request → Assess requirements
  • Assess requirements → Check service coverage
  • Check service coverage → Match supply & demand
  • Contractor acquisition → Contractor onboarding
  • Contractor onboarding → Contractor activation
  • Contractor activation → Match supply & demand
  • Match supply & demand → Coordinate communication
  • Coordinate communication → Confirm the booking
  • Confirm the booking → Operations management
  • Coordinate communication → Exception management
  • Confirm the booking → Exception management
  • Exception management → Match supply & demand
  • Exception management → Operations management

AI Customer Communications

An AI-powered communication system that manages customer inquiries, conversational interactions, appointment scheduling and customer reactivation.

What it is

An AI-powered customer communication system that combines intelligent inquiry management, conversational voice and SMS agents, appointment scheduling and customer reactivation.

The system helps businesses respond to customer needs, capture opportunities and manage routine conversations while reducing dependence on manual intervention.

Business challenges

  • Missed calls and unanswered customer inquiries.
  • Slow response times resulting in missed opportunities.
  • Repetitive conversations consuming employee time.
  • Limited customer service availability outside business hours.
  • Time-consuming manual appointment management.
  • Underutilized customer databases.
  • Fragmented communication across multiple channels.

Business value

  • Faster and more consistent customer responses.
  • Extended customer communication availability.
  • Reduced administrative workload.
  • Better utilization of existing customer databases.
  • More efficient appointment management.
  • Increased communication capacity without proportional staffing increases.

My Contribution

I led the project end-to-end, from the initial communication problem through planning, implementation, testing and release.

Problem

Slow or inconsistent responses were losing inquiries, while booking commitments needed to respect service duration and calendar availability.

What I did

Defined the inquiry triage and appointment rules: how inquiries were captured, prioritized and routed, and when a voice or SMS response could offer or change a time. Built and tested the AI voice and SMS workflows, including booking, rescheduling and cancellation paths, then prepared the system for release.

Scope

The voice, SMS and reactivation examples were separate workflows within one communication system; I owned the project from idea through shipping.

How the work flows
  1. 01
    Incoming inquiries

    Capture new customer interest.

    Collect incoming customer inquiries and relevant contact information, including the source of the inquiry where available. This helps businesses organize incoming demand and reduce the likelihood of overlooking potential opportunities.

  2. 02
    Customer reactivation

    Reconnect with existing prospects.

    Identify previous customers and inactive prospects who may still benefit from the business’s services. Initiate relevant conversations to uncover opportunities within the existing customer database instead of relying exclusively on new customer acquisition.

  3. 03
    Understand customer needs

    Qualify and prioritize inquiries.

    Evaluate the nature, intent and relevance of each inquiry. Determine what the customer needs and which communication or follow-up approach is appropriate, allowing the business to allocate attention more effectively.

  4. 04
    AI voice agent

    Handle natural phone conversations.

    Enable customers to interact with an AI-powered voice receptionist capable of understanding inquiries and supporting routine customer interactions, including appointment-related requests. This reduces dependence on staff availability for routine telephone conversations.

  5. 05
    AI SMS agent

    Engage customers through text.

    Use conversational AI to interpret customer messages, provide relevant responses and continue interactions through SMS. Customers can communicate asynchronously while the business maintains a more consistent response process.

  6. 06
    Determine next action

    Turn conversations into decisions.

    Identify the appropriate next step based on the customer’s request, conversation and available information. This may involve answering a question, managing an appointment, continuing the conversation or identifying a need for human assistance.

  7. 07
    Appointment management

    Book, reschedule or cancel.

    Support appointment-related requests by checking calendar availability and managing bookings, rescheduling or cancellations. This reduces administrative work and helps customers complete routine scheduling tasks more conveniently.

  8. 08
    Follow-up & human handoff

    Continue or escalate conversations.

    Identify conversations requiring additional attention and determine whether further communication or human involvement is appropriate. The objective is to prevent opportunities from being overlooked while recognizing the limits of automated communication.

  9. 09
    CRM & communication tracking

    Maintain customer interaction visibility.

    Organize customer information, inquiry status, communication history and appointment activity. Give the business a clearer understanding of ongoing conversations and outstanding customer needs.

B.R.A.I.N.

Business Knowledge Assistant

An organizational knowledge system that transforms business documentation, operating procedures and institutional knowledge into accessible, AI-powered business intelligence.

What it is

An AI-powered organizational knowledge system designed to help businesses centralize, organize and access internal knowledge.

B.R.A.I.N. combines standard operating procedures, business documentation, playbooks and organizational memory with an AI knowledge assistant designed to retrieve relevant information and provide company-specific guidance.

Its objective is to make organizational knowledge more accessible, actionable and useful across the business.

Business challenges

  • Business knowledge scattered across multiple documents and platforms.
  • Employees spending excessive time searching for information.
  • Repeated questions consuming management time.
  • Inconsistent execution of business procedures.
  • Loss of institutional knowledge when employees leave.
  • Time-consuming employee onboarding.
  • Generic AI assistants lacking company-specific context.

Business value

  • Centralized organizational intelligence.
  • Faster access to relevant business information.
  • More consistent execution of company procedures.
  • Reduced dependence on individual employees’ knowledge.
  • More efficient employee training and onboarding.
  • Company-specific AI assistance.
  • Better preservation of institutional knowledge.

My Contribution

I worked across the knowledge structure and the assistant experience, from defining how guidance was organized to building onboarding, chat, playbooks and organizational memory.

Problem

Knowledge was scattered across documents and platforms, slowing onboarding and making routine answers depend on whoever was available.

What I did

Defined the operating-library structure and document-access boundaries for procedures, decisions and playbooks. Implemented the assistant’s onboarding and chat experience, including the organization-scoped path that retrieved available documents and memory, and added playbook and organizational-memory features.

Scope

The operating library and retrieval prototype remained separate systems; I worked across their knowledge structure and the assistant experience.

How the work flows
  1. 01
    Business documentation

    Capture how the business operates.

    Collect standard operating procedures, company policies, sales playbooks and operational guidelines. Transform scattered business documentation into a structured organizational resource.

  2. 02
    Organizational memory

    Preserve business knowledge and experience.

    Document important decisions, established practices, business strategies and lessons learned. Preserve valuable institutional knowledge so that it does not remain exclusively with individual employees or managers.

  3. 03
    Organize business knowledge

    Create a structured knowledge library.

    Organize business information into relevant categories, departments, procedures and playbooks. Establish a consistent structure that makes information easier to maintain, locate and understand.

  4. 04
    Maintain & update knowledge

    Keep organizational information relevant.

    Track changes to business documentation and maintain updated procedures as the organization evolves. Help distinguish established operating practices from outdated information.

  5. 05
    Employee question

    Ask questions in natural language.

    Allow employees to ask questions about company procedures, responsibilities, sales practices or operational requirements without manually searching through multiple documents.

  6. 06
    Retrieve relevant knowledge

    Find the information that matters.

    Identify relevant passages within the assistant’s available knowledge sources based on the employee’s question. Retrieval-augmented generation helps provide company-specific context rather than relying exclusively on a general AI model’s knowledge.

  7. 07
    AI-powered business assistance

    Provide contextual business guidance.

    Generate responses grounded in retrieved organizational information, within the appropriate organizational access boundaries. Help employees understand procedures, locate relevant guidance and navigate business processes.

  8. 08
    Informed decisions & execution

    Turn knowledge into practical action.

    Help employees make better-informed decisions, follow established procedures and complete routine tasks with greater independence. The objective is to improve organizational consistency while reducing dependence on management for routine guidance.

Safiy

Contextual AI Workplace Assistant

A local-first AI workplace assistant that transforms conversations, professional context and knowledge into intelligent assistance and business actions.

What it is

Safiy is a local-first AI workplace assistant designed to capture conversations, organize professional knowledge, provide contextual assistance and support actions across connected applications.

By combining meeting intelligence, knowledge retrieval, real-time AI assistance and application integrations, Safiy bridges the gap between workplace conversations and business execution.

Its objective is to make AI a contextual part of everyday professional workflows rather than a separate tool users must repeatedly provide with information.

Business challenges

  • Valuable information lost after meetings.
  • Excessive time spent taking notes and preparing summaries.
  • Difficulty retrieving information from previous conversations.
  • Frequent switching between business applications.
  • Manual execution of post-meeting administrative tasks.
  • Generic AI assistants lacking relevant workplace context.
  • Privacy concerns surrounding sensitive business conversations.

Business value

  • Improved meeting productivity.
  • Better retention and accessibility of business knowledge.
  • Reduced post-meeting administrative workload.
  • More informed professional conversations.
  • Greater continuity between discussions and business actions.
  • Reduced application switching.
  • Greater control over sensitive information.

My Contribution

I worked on the knowledge and release layer of Safiy, building on the open-source Meeting Transcriber project.

Problem

Meeting context is easy to lose, but uploading recordings can create privacy and adoption barriers.

What I did

Added Knowledge Bank imports, improved retrieval indexing and prepared beta releases.

Scope

I worked within a local-first direction — recording and transcription on the Mac by default, with connected services treated as explicit choices. The broader assistant, connected actions and suggested tasks extend beyond the features I built.

How the work flows
  1. 01
    Capture conversations

    Capture workplace discussions.

    Record and transcribe professional conversations across virtual and in-person meetings. Transform spoken information into searchable content that can be reviewed and used beyond the original conversation.

  2. 02
    Connect knowledge

    Bring relevant information together.

    Organize documents, reference materials, previous conversations and project-specific information through Knowledge Banks and Workspaces. Give the AI assistant access to relevant professional context rather than relying exclusively on general knowledge.

  3. 03
    Understand context

    Identify relevant workplace information.

    Use conversation content, meeting history and available knowledge to establish context around the user’s current activity. Retrieve relevant information to support more meaningful assistance.

  4. 04
    Contextual AI assistant

    Provide intelligence around your work.

    Combine available professional context with AI capabilities to help users understand information, explore ideas, answer questions and identify potential next steps. Reduce the need to repeatedly explain the same business context to an AI assistant.

  5. 05
    Real-time assistance

    Support conversations as they happen.

    Provide contextual suggestions, relevant answers, preparation guidance and potential action recommendations during professional conversations. Help users remain focused and better informed without constantly switching between applications.

  6. 06
    Business action execution

    Turn discussions into business actions.

    Support actions across connected applications such as Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Docs, Sheets and Drive. Help translate meeting discussions into practical tasks, documents, email drafts and scheduled activities, subject to supported integrations and user permissions.

  7. 07
    Preserve workplace knowledge

    Retain valuable professional context.

    Organize meeting transcripts, conversation history and related information within accessible workspaces. Allow users to retrieve previous discussions and relevant knowledge without manually reconstructing past events.

  8. 08
    Continuous intelligence

    Carry knowledge into future work.

    Make accumulated professional knowledge available to support future conversations, questions and activities. Create continuity between past discussions and present work while maintaining user control over stored information.
